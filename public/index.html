01What it is, in your words

It knows my money.

Twelve questions, about five minutes, in your own words or a voice note. Charles reads your money back to you in one line and you say if it is right before anything is built on it.

A 90-day plan from moves that work.

Thirteen fixed moves, each with the evidence for it. Charles picks the ones your answers earned and puts your numbers in. It cannot invent a step, a fee or a deadline.

One step at a time.

WhatsApp sends the next step, how to do it, and a “Done” button. A few days later it asks whether it helped. The plan only moves when you say it moved.

One rule I live by.

Not a course. One sentence you would say to yourself, chosen by you from three, against what people in your situation usually do instead.

“The mashonisa gets paid first. Nothing new gets borrowed.”

One rule a person might choose. It is theirs, in their words, and Charles holds them to it for ninety days.

02Why Charles is built this way

Tips don’t work. Rules do.

South Africa has had a national financial-education policy, a Financial Literacy Month and every bank’s money tips for a decade. The score has not moved. Three findings decide what Charles is.

Tips don’t work.

TymeBank itself launched a financial-literacy SMS service with Juntos in 2020 — budgeting, saving and credit tips — and an education app, TymeCoach, with a chatbot. The national literacy score did not move: the FSCA’s baseline puts about half of adults as financially literate, and it has been flat for years. People do not lack the knowledge that a mashonisa is expensive. They lack the next step, in their numbers, this week, and someone who asks on Friday whether it happened.

FSCA baseline ~51%: FSCA / Treasury draft National Consumer Financial Education Policy (2026), via secondary reporting.

Rules of thumb beat lessons.

A randomised trial in the Dominican Republic compared standard accounting training with a simple rule-of-thumb course: separate the money physically, one rule at a time. The rules improved financial practices and revenues where the training did nothing, and the gains were largest for the people with the weakest starting practices.

Drexler, Fischer & Schoar, Keeping It Simple, American Economic Journal: Applied Economics, 2014.

Menus hurt the weakest.

A Harvard Business School field experiment gave 640 Kenyan entrepreneurs an AI mentor on WhatsApp that offered three to five suggestions per question. No average effect. Underneath the average, the strongest gained 15–20% and the weakest did about 10% worse, because they could not tell which suggestion fitted them. The usual chatbot makes the people who need it most worse off.

Otis, Clarke, Delecourt, Holtz & Koning, HBS Working Paper 24-042.

So Charles gives one recommendation. Never a menu. Never a loan.

The customer is not illiterate. She is overwhelmed.

Picture a nurse’s aide on R7,800 with two children, a mother to support, a clothing account, a bank loan and a mashonisa. She does not need a course. She needs one rule — the mashonisa gets paid first, nothing new gets borrowed — and a plan that fits her payday. That is the unit Charles works in: one household, one rule, one step this week.

03How it goes

Five minutes of questions. Ninety days of steps.

  1. First, a hardship check — before anything else.

    If you cannot cover food, rent or transport, or you are borrowing to eat, Charles does not plan. It gives three moves — borrow nothing more, write down every rand owed, make one free call — names the free help, and pauses with a way back.

  2. Twelve questions, about five minutes.

    What to call you, how money comes in, what you are trying to fix, what is in the way, who depends on you, roughly what comes in, who you owe, what you could reach tomorrow, what cover you have, how you keep track.

  3. Charles reads your money back. You confirm it.

    One line about your money life, established once and reused — never re-read from a voice note that might have misheard you.

  4. You choose the goal.

    Three genuinely different 90-day goals your answers support, each with what it is worth in your own numbers and what you could do this week. Charles recommends one and says why.

  5. You choose the rule.

    Three one-sentence rules of thumb, shown next to what usually happens instead — borrow again, skip a payment, ask family — so the rule is chosen against reality.

  6. The plan, as one document.

    Where your money stands. The strategy. The next 90 days in order. It prints, it downloads, and it only ever says what you actually told Charles.

  7. Then one step at a time.

    Send. Do. Done. Did it help? Charles follows up on each step and the plan changes when your month does — within bounds, never freestyled.

04The Money Map

Eight areas. What sorted means in each.

Every household is read the same way, against the same ruler. An area only moves when you show Charles it moved.

Money in

You know what comes in and the day it lands, and every grant or benefit you qualify for is claimed.

Money out

A month plan exists that fits the money, and bills are paid on the day money lands.

What you owe

Every rand owed is listed, the costliest is being paid first, nothing new borrowed, no mashonisa.

Emergency money

A named amount you can reach without borrowing, kept apart, growing on payday.

Credit record

Report checked in the last year, errors disputed, every account paid on time for three months.

Cover

One funeral policy that fits, no duplicates, beneficiaries named; the people who depend on you are covered.

Family money

Support to family is a fixed line everyone knows; the stokvel or burial society is one you can verify.

The future

A written 90-day goal with a number on it, and a longer one with a vehicle you have started.

Four money stages, read from the same answers: surviving (cannot make the month), stretching (makes the month, nothing kept), steady (a small cushion, debts under control), building (saving toward a goal).

06Twelve promises

The rules Charles keeps.

Each one came from a real failure in the product Charles is built from. They are written into the code and tested, not into a policy.

  1. The plan comes from a fixed library, never freestyled.

    Thirteen moves with the evidence for them. Charles chooses which you earned and puts your numbers in. It cannot invent a step.

  2. It never claims you said something you did not.

    No “the problem you mentioned” unless you mentioned it.

  3. One recommendation, never a menu.

    Charles picks, and says what would change the pick.

  4. You choose the goal and the rule.

    From three genuinely different options each, with what usually happens instead printed next to them.

  5. Your money is read back once and confirmed.

    Then that confirmed version is what every later step uses.

  6. Every figure comes from a verified, dated source.

    A fee, a rule, a grant amount, a deadline. Past 90 days, it is hedged to “confirm first”. Where sources disagree, Charles says so.

  7. Free and reversible before anything that costs.

    In every recommendation.

  8. Hardship mode.

    If you cannot cover food, rent or transport, Charles stops planning, protects cash, and points to free help.

  9. It never recommends credit.

    No loan, no buy-now-pay-later, no cash advance, however cheap. If you are considering one, Charles works through your numbers and says the downside first.

  10. It never asks for an ID number, account number, card number, PIN, OTP or password.

    And says so in the first message. Charles reads no statement and no app; it knows what you tell it.

  11. “Forget my money” deletes everything.

    One message, any time.

  12. Your own AI can connect.

    ChatGPT or Claude can read the same plan and memory through a connector, and anything it suggests changing is confirmed with you on WhatsApp first.

07Where it stands

No cohort has run yet.

There are no testimonials because there are no users. No logos, no “trusted by”, no pilot results. What is on this page is the method and the evidence it is built on; what is not on it is a single real person’s ninety days.

What exists

  • The whole journey, on a WhatsApp test number: hello to diagnostic to read-back to goal to rule to plan to first step.
  • A South African library of rights, rules and places to go, each row with the date it was checked.
  • The plan as a printable document, and a page to read it on the web.
  • A connector for the person’s own AI.

What does not, on purpose

  • Reading bank statements or the bank’s data. Charles knows what it is told.
  • A second market’s library. Nobody has verified another country’s rules yet.
  • A recommendation to take credit, ever.
  • Testimonials, user counts, pilot results.

08For the bank

Why put this on a bank’s WhatsApp number.

You already tried content.

The Juntos SMS tips and the TymeCoach app were the right instinct with the wrong unit: the message, not the household. Charles is a plan with follow-through — a step, a how-to, a “done”, a “did it help” — for one household at a time, on the number your customers already have.

The rails already exist.

A plan step for a GoTyme customer is not advice. “Move R50 to GoalSave on grant day” is a thing the bank’s own app does, for free. Charles sits on top of what you have built and turns it into habits. It names a free, reversible product of yours where it fits; it never pushes a loan.

A stabilised customer can later be lent to responsibly.

Arrears cleared, a cushion that grows on payday, a bank-visible record of behaviour: that is a customer who could not be underwritten yesterday. Charles promises no outcome. The measure we would propose is the bank’s own — the share of customers who become credit-worthy — and the Kenya evidence says the first thing to prove is that the weakest do no worse.

Doing no harm is the feature.

Hardship mode pushes no product, names the free debt counsellor and the ombud, and pauses the plan. That is what earns the trust a lending relationship needs.

Your data could feed it later. That is your call.

The same door a person’s own AI uses is a door the bank’s systems could use. Nothing is read from the bank today. It is the obvious next step, and it is deliberately not built until you decide it should be.

What a pilot needs from the bank

  • A WhatsApp number on the bank’s business account.
  • A pilot cohort, invited by the bank.
  • Nothing from the core systems at first. Charles knows only what each person tells it.
Not on this page

Pricing, outcome targets, and anything Charles has not done yet. The pilot would set the measures before anyone talks about scale.

Talk about a pilot

See it before a pilot: the chat, on an invented household · a full plan document · the cohort report a bank would read. All three run on made-up data and say so.
